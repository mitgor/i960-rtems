# AcceleRAID 160 external-programmer recovery package

Prepared offline 2026-11-16. This is a verification checklist for a later bench session, not an instruction to program either card now. The first spare still has the initial native image that failed the HP Z400 pre-beep test. The unmodified second spare is the stock reference. Keep each physical chip, original image, candidate, and programmer file identified by **card**, not by a common flash-chip filename.

## Chip contract and remaining physical unknowns

The photographed first-spare chip marking was read as **Intel E28F008S3-120**. Intel's [Byte-Wide Smart 3 FlashFile family datasheet](https://datasheet4u.com/pdf/1183856/28F008S3.pdf) identifies the `008` density as 8 Mbit = 1,048,576 × 8 bits, `E` as commercial 40-lead TSOP, and `-120` as the 120 ns grade at 3.3 V ± 0.3 V VCC. It provides a **standard TSOP top-view** pin diagram. Intel describes read-array operation after power-up, identifier/status modes, sixteen 64 KiB erase blocks, and separate `VPP`; program/erase options include low-voltage VPP and 12 V VPP. The programmer must use the **exact S3 device/profile** and its verified voltage and timing settings. The existence of a 12 V option is not a reason to select it by default. [Intel datasheet, family overview and ordering table](https://datasheet4u.com/pdf/1183856/28F008S3.pdf).

Still **unverified on the physical cards**: the first chip's pin-1 direction relative to the socket/adapter and board pads; the second chip's exact top marking/package; the adapter's TSOP-40 footprint and whether its pinout view is top or bottom; the board's VCC and VPP during boot/programming; block-lock state; whether any in-circuit clip would back-power the 80303 or contend with its address/data bus; the programmer's selected part profile, firmware and supply levels; and solder-joint health after chip handling. Before any connection, photograph both chip faces, pin-1 marks and adapter/socket orientation, then match the 40-pin S3 datasheet diagram to the exact adapter drawing. Do not copy the pinout of an SA, SC, S5, reverse-pinout TSOP, or a different density. Intel's bus-operation table makes `RP#`, `CE#`, `OE#`, `WE#`, `RY/BY#`, `VCC` and `VPP` material to safe operation. [Intel bus operations](https://datasheet4u.com/pdf/1183856/28F008S3.pdf).

## Bench checklist: first-spare stock restoration

1. **Identify and isolate.** Label the failed first spare and chip, photograph both sides and pin 1, record programmer model/firmware, exact selected `E28F008S3-120` profile, adapter part and orientation. Power the PC/card off and disconnect it. Prefer a removed chip in a correctly matched socket. If an in-circuit method is proposed, first prove electrical isolation, no back-power, no bus contention and permissible VCC/VPP on the actual board; otherwise stop.
2. **Preserve the current state.** Before erase/program, perform two full *independent* programmer reads of the first chip, save raw files without overwriting `programmer-read-0.bin`, record size and SHA-256, compare the two reads byte for byte and to `6f16d713...`. A mismatch means stop and investigate contact, orientation, power or chip damage. Record the chip identifier/lock-bit information if the exact programmer profile can read it, with the caveat that identifier-mode reads are not a substitute for full-array reads.
3. **Verify the intended recovery source.** Run the local image verifier. Independently hash `restore-first-spare-stock-1MiB.bin` and check `fadd2b0f...`; compare it bytewise to both saved preflash stock reads. Keep a second copy outside the working directory. Do not use the second spare's stock file.
4. **Only at a later authorized restore session:** use the documented programmer S3 erase/program/verify procedure, including block-lock handling and the profile's specified VCC/VPP. Do not improvise a voltage from the chip marking. After its built-in verify, power-cycle the programmer and make **two new full independent reads**; compare both bytes to the first-spare stock recovery image. Save the raw readbacks, programmer log, chip ID, profile and voltage settings.
5. **Physical acceptance after verified restoration:** inspect solder/adapter connections, install the restored first spare in Slot 6 with the original known-good card in Slot 5 (the configuration used for the failed POST), and record HP F10 settings and POST behavior. A beep and Linux boot plus expected PCI identity and a fresh guarded full-flash read of the restored spare would establish stock recovery. If it still hangs, the remaining hypothesis includes chip/card electrical damage or a programmer/assembly problem; do not treat the native firmware as the sole cause.

The first three items are preparation and evidence preservation; item 4 is a future **write** step and is not authorized by this document. A successful programmer verify without the independent power-cycled reads and stock cold boot is incomplete recovery evidence.

## Second spare: candidate gate, separate from recovery

Keep the second spare stock until the first-spare failure has a useful discriminator or an SRAM/stock fallback path is proven. Before any decision about its candidate, repeat the two guarded READIMAGE captures from the **second** spare, ensure they still match `0ca0f4b6...`, confirm its exact chip marking and orientation, rerun the image verifier against the *current* candidate hash, and capture its own full programmer backup if the chip is removed. The candidate must preserve that second spare's descriptor. A passing five-request QEMU gate or Linux login prompt is not a physical POST or `myrs` driver-attach test; see the [model-assumption audit](ACCELE160-EMULATOR-ASSUMPTIONS.md).

No currently saved report proves the first spare has been restored to stock, that the second-spare candidate boots on HP hardware, or that the external programmer setup is electrically qualified for either card. The read-only [POST failure audit](ACCELE160-POST-FAILURE-AUDIT.md) lists the measurements that could separate firmware startup from electrical/BIOS failure.
